# Using the Advatar CRM

A short guide for the team. Nothing technical.

---

## Signing in

Go to the CRM address and sign in with your email and password. Where
you land depends on your role — you don't need to pick anything.

The **sun/moon button** in the top right switches between light and
dark. Your choice is remembered on that device.

The **magnifying glass** (or just pressing `/`) searches every client
and lead from anywhere. Start typing a name and press it.

---

## The roles

| Role | What they see |
|---|---|
| **CEO** | Everything: all clients, all money, staff payments, and who's on the team. |
| **Operations Manager** | Sales and strategy: all clients, the pipeline, prospects, and invoices. Not staff wages, not each client's monthly value, and can't invite people. |
| **Staff** | Only the clients they've been assigned to. No money at all. |
| **Videographer** | Only their assigned clients, read-only, except that they add each video's link (new versions too, and correct a wrong link), tick off the changes asked for, tick a video as posted and add where it was posted. The CEO or operations approve each video before the client sees it. Plus their own payments. |
| **Client** | Only their own project portal. |

**Important:** a staff member genuinely cannot reach another client's
information — not by searching, not by guessing a link. That's enforced
in the database, not just hidden in the menus. To give someone access
to a client, open that client and add them under **Assigned Team**.

## Day to day

### Dashboard
Your morning screen. **Needs attention** at the top is the list to
clear: overdue invoices, overdue follow-ups, tasks due today, and
clients nobody has spoken to in three weeks. Everything on it clicks
through to the thing itself.

### Client dashboard and deadlines
Opening a client lands on its **Dashboard**: what needs doing now, what's
coming up in the next four weeks, and every video with its deadline.

- **The rule:** each video should be **sent for approval by the Friday of
  the week before it goes out**. The latest it can ever be is **3 days
  before** it goes out.
- Every dated video gets four to-dos automatically: **Film** (Monday of
  that week), **Edit** (Thursday), **Send for approval** (Friday) and
  **Post** (the day). They go to the videographer on the client, move
  when the video moves, and tick themselves as the work happens.
- They're ordinary to-dos: the same ones show on **My dashboard** and
  **To-do**, and ticking one anywhere ticks it everywhere. Ticking
  **Post** marks the video as posted.
- The **weeks-ahead meter** is green at 2+ weeks ahead (the goal), amber
  when a video has missed its target, red within 3 days of going out.
  Every client's meter is also on the front dashboard, worst first.

### Scripts
Each client has a **Scripts** tab. Write a script, then either put it on
a planned video (its hook, body and call to action are copied onto the
video, and later edits follow it) or leave it in the library for later.
Clients never see the library.

### YOU (your own content)
Next to the **Clients** title, **YOU** opens your own content: pick
**Personal brand** or **The business**, then it's the same dashboard as
any client. These never appear in the client list, Finance or the
pipeline. Switch someone on under **Settings → Team access → Your
content** to let them work on it.

### Guidelines: Filming and Editing
**Tools → Guidelines** has two sections, **Filming** and **Editing** (and
**General** for any checklist not put in either yet: use its **Section**
menu to move it).

Down the side are **your clients and their content pillars** (from each
client's content plan). Click a pillar to see **how to film** or **how to
edit** that kind of video: the **elements needed** and the **step-by-step
process**. A filled dot means that guide is written.

- **Create guide** starts from the Filming or Editing template (the CEO
  can change the templates at the bottom of each section). Write it with
  the videographer when you make the first video for that pillar.
- **Start a new video** gives a fresh checklist to tick through, linked to
  a planned video if you like. Its progress shows on the client's
  Dashboard ("How to film this 2/5").
- Every video on the Dashboard and in Videos has **How to film this** and
  **How to edit this** links. The CEO, ops manager and the team on that
  client can edit guides; clients never see them.

### Your Journey
**Your Journey** (in the menu for everyone on the team) shows each person
their path: where they started, **You are here**, and every level ahead
out to the long-term vision (your own office). Each level shows what it
means, what you can do, a **client range** and an **earnings range in £**
(always a range), and a **To move up** checklist.

- There are two paths to start with: **Videographer** and **Sales**. Put
  each person on one under **Your Journey → Team journeys**.
- People tick their own steps; they show as **Waiting for sign-off** until
  you or an ops manager open them and press **Sign off**. Moving someone
  up a level (**Move … to this level**) records the date.
- Anyone can add videos to their **video progress**: where they started,
  what they make now, and videos they want to make next. The section only
  shows once something's in it.
- **Edit the paths** (CEO only) changes every level, range and checklist.
  It also holds a private **minimum level** marker per path that only the
  CEO ever sees; the team only sees "First goal: get paid".

### Pipeline
Your leads, in three columns: **Leads** (not pitched yet), **Proposal**
(pitched, waiting), and **Recently won**.

- **Weighted pipeline** at the top is what you're likely to actually
  land — each lead's value multiplied by how likely it is. A lead with
  no likelihood set counts as 50%.
- Anything past its follow-up date is **outlined in red**.
- **Log contact** records that you spoke to them and sets the next
  follow-up in one go. Use this every time you talk to a lead — it's
  what keeps the red outlines honest.
- **→ Proposal** and **→ Won** move a lead along.

### Clients
Everyone you work with, leads included. Search and filter at the top.
**+ Add client** creates a new one — set the stage to "Lead" if they're
not signed yet.

### A client's page
- **Info** — their details, notes, and (if you're CEO) their monthly value.
- **Onboarding** — a checklist created automatically when a client
  becomes Active. Tick items off. You can add your own steps, and apply
  a **task template** to create a whole set of dated tasks at once.
- **Finance** — their invoices and what they owe. CEO and ops manager only.
- **Tasks** — day-to-day work for this client.
- **Documents** — upload briefs, contracts, brand guidelines. Files are
  private: only your team and that client can open them.
- **Meetings** — attach a Fathom recording. Paste the link and the
  summary; if they have no 90-day plan yet, one gets drafted from it.
- **90-Day Plan** tab — their content plan.
- **Activity** tab — everything that's ever happened to this client.

### Finance
Money coming in. Invoices sorted with what needs chasing at the top.

- **Draft** — you're still preparing it.
- **Sent** — it's with the client, waiting.
- **Paid** — done.
- **Overdue** — sent, and the due date has passed. Worked out
  automatically; you never set it.

**Mark sent** and **Mark paid** are one click from the list.

### Payments
Separate from Finance, and the other direction: what you pay staff and
videographers. CEO only. Staff see just their own under **My Payments**.

### This week
The last seven days on one screen: new leads, deals won, invoices
raised and paid, tasks completed, and what's overdue right now.

### Prospects
Discovery calls that Fathom sent in automatically and nobody has
reviewed yet. Open each one and check it before acting on it — nothing
in there has been confirmed by a person.

### Team
Invite a new login and set their role. CEO only. There's also a link
here to **Task templates** — repeatable sets of tasks (a shoot cycle,
an onboarding run) your team can apply to any client in one click.

## Habits that make it work

The CRM is only as good as what goes into it. Three things matter most:

1. **Log contact every time you speak to a lead.** Everything about
   chasing depends on it.
2. **Set a follow-up date whenever you leave something open.** It
   creates a task automatically, so it can't quietly disappear.
3. **Mark invoices sent and paid as it happens.** Otherwise the money
   figures — and the overdue list — are fiction.

## Performance: followers, platforms and growth

On a client's **Performance** tab:

- **Platform** buttons sit next to the time picker. *All platforms* is the
  default; pick one (say TikTok) to see only its numbers. **Create report**
  makes the report for whatever is showing.
- **Followers** shows the count at the start of the time chosen and at the
  end, with the change. For a month (e.g. Month 1, 10 Jul to 9 Aug) it's the
  count on 10 Jul to the count on 9 Aug. "All time" starts from the count
  entered for the day we started.
- **Growth over time** is one graph: pick Views, Followers, Likes, Comments
  or Shares above it, Days or Months, and *Running total* (adds up from the
  start) or *Each day/month*. Hover for the numbers; *Show as a table* lists
  them all.
- Posts read from the profile that aren't one of the planned videos are
  counted too, marked "TikTok post" (or Instagram etc.) with a link to them.

## Professional Replier (Messages)

A tab on **Messages** for the CEO and anyone the CEO switches on (**Team access → Professional Replier**). Clients never see it.

1. Choose the client (or "Not a client yet"), who's replying (Sales, Videographer, Operations, CEO) and where (WhatsApp by default, Instagram DM, email or text).
2. Paste the client's message. Add a note for the replier if it helps ("we can only film Tuesdays this month").
3. Press **Write replies**. You get the **best reply** and two other options, each with a **Copy** button. Anything to fill in first (a date in [brackets]) is listed under *Check before sending*.

It knows the client's package, this month, the next videos and when they're planned to be filmed, and the recent portal chat. It never makes up dates, prices or promises.

**Needs the CEO first.** When a message touches something on the CEO's list (going ahead with another month, contracts, prices, refunds, complaints, cancelling, extra work...), it says so. In that case:

- The replies are holding replies that don't agree to anything.
- The CEO gets a to-do and a notification.
- The CEO sees it under **Waiting for you**, with a short note on what needs deciding.

**Getting better.** Tap 👍 or 👎 on each set of replies. The CEO can:

- Press **Teach it** under any reply to save it as a good example.
- Edit the **Playbook**: what needs the CEO, the house style, things never to promise, and facts about Advatar.

The replies follow both from then on. The cost of each use is on **Finance**, under *Professional Replier*.

## Team walkthroughs (sales and videography)

The first time someone on the sales team (a "staff" login) or the videography team signs in, they see:

1. **A welcome**: four short screens with an inspiring message for their team.
2. **A guided tour** of the pages they actually have. It skips anything they can't open, like Passwords if that switch is off, or the Professional Replier if you haven't given it to them.
3. **"You're all set"**, with their first three things to do.

It shows once per person. They can replay the tour, or just the welcome, from the ⓘ button in the bottom corner. Operations managers and the CEO don't get one.

**See it yourself:** on **Team access**, press **Preview sales** or **Preview videography**. Watching a preview doesn't use up anyone's walkthrough.

The wording lives in `lib/team-tour.tsx`. Ask for changes there.

## Proposals and payment (Prospects)

A proposal is a link you send a prospect. It opens as an animated slideshow in the CRM's own style.

**What the client sees:**
1. **The packages:** Essentials, Growth (shown first) and Accelerator, as pictures with no prices.
2. **One page per part, in order**, each showing the visuals first and the price breakdown at the bottom:
   - High Quality Visuals;
   - Strategy and planning;
   - Shoots;
   - Edits;
   - Social media management;
   - Ad Campaigns (Meta, TikTok, Google, posters and leaflets);
   - Influencer Marketing.

   They can add extras (an extra shoot hour, a full day, more videos and so on), and the total updates as they go.
3. **The summary:**
   - three big tabs;
   - Monthly, or 3 months upfront at 15% off;
   - the total;
   - **Let's go**;
   - the **View the contract** link, with an arrow pointing to it.
4. **Let's go:**
   - they type their email (already filled in if you have it);
   - the CRM saves their acceptance of the contract and emails them the proposal, contract and invoice;
   - they choose **card** (Stripe) or **cash**.

They have to go through every page before **Let's go** works. Ad spend and printing are never in our price: the ad pages show a *suggested* budget that they pay directly.

**Making one (CEO, operations and sales):**
1. **Prospects → Proposals → + New proposal.** Choose a new business or an existing client, add the contact name and email if you have them, and pick a template.
2. **Edit anything**, on the left:
   - every heading and line;
   - the Cloudinary links for pictures and videos (empty ones show a placeholder);
   - the hours and prices in each package;
   - the extras;
   - the suggested ad budgets;
   - the hourly rate (£35);
   - the 3-month discount (15%);
   - the contract;
   - the message.
3. **Preview** shows exactly what they'll see. Nothing in a preview is tracked.
4. **Save as template** keeps this setup to start future proposals from.
5. On the deal page, **Create link / Copy link** copies the link. **Copy message** copies the ready-written email, with the link in it, to send from your Advatar email.

**Changing the offer:** on the deal page, **New version from this** copies the current version so you can edit it and send the new link. The old link then says "This proposal has been updated" and points to the new one. The client goes through every page again.

**What you'll see on the deal:**
- whether they opened it, and how many times;
- whether they read it all;
- which packages they looked at, and for how long;
- time on each page, with the longest highlighted;
- which breakdowns they read and which extras they ticked;
- whether they opened the contract;
- how far they got: Let's go, email, card or cash.

You're notified when they open it, finish it, accept it and pay.

**When they pay:**
- **Card:** Stripe confirms the payment, and the CRM then:
  - marks it paid;
  - makes them a client, with a paid invoice and their billing (monthly);
  - creates their login and emails it to them;
  - puts them under **Needs a team** on Prospects for the CEO to assign.

  Monthly card payments carry on automatically.
- **Cash:** the client is told to pay cash before you start. Once you have it, press **Cash received** on the deal, and they're set up the same way. Note later monthly cash payments there too.
- **Existing clients** on a new deal are updated. No second client or login is made.
- **Get login details** on the deal makes a fresh temporary password to hand over, if their email didn't arrive.

### Switching on card payments (Stripe, one-time setup)
1. In Stripe, go to **Developers → API keys → Create restricted key**. Give it:
   - **Write:** Checkout Sessions, Products, Prices and Customers.
   - **Read:** Subscriptions and Invoices.

   Copy the key (it starts `rk_live_`).
2. In Stripe, go to **Developers → Webhooks → Add endpoint**:
   - URL: `https://<your-site>/api/stripe/webhook`
   - Events: `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded` and `invoice.paid`.

   Copy the **signing secret** (it starts `whsec_`).
3. In Vercel, go to **Settings → Environment Variables**, add `STRIPE_CHECKOUT_KEY` (the `rk_` key) and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (the `whsec_` secret), then redeploy.

Until this is done, clients only see the cash option, and the deal page says so.

The contract, invoice and login emails need email set up: `RESEND_API_KEY` and `EMAIL_FROM`, the same as for notifications. Without it, everything else still works. The deal page tells you to send the contract and invoice links yourself, and **Get login details** gives you their password to hand over.

The 3 months upfront option is a one-off Stripe payment. After the 3 months, set up their monthly billing (or a new proposal) as you agree with them.
